How to get a full-screen meeting alert on Mac

By Nicolas Frugoni, who builds MeetingFlash.

If you work with headphones on and a document full-screen, the little banner macOS Calendar shows in the corner is easy to miss. It slides in, sits for a few seconds and leaves. This guide covers why that happens, what you can change for free in macOS, and when a full-screen alert is the better fix.

Why Calendar alerts get missed

  • Banners disappear. Unless you set the style to Alerts in System Settings, Notifications, Calendar, a banner dismisses itself.
  • Focus modes hide them. Do Not Disturb or a work Focus can suppress Calendar notifications entirely.
  • They're small and quiet. A corner banner doesn't compete with the thing you're concentrating on.

Step 1: make macOS notifications harder to ignore

  1. Open System Settings, Notifications, Calendar.
  2. Set the style to Alerts so the notification stays until you act on it.
  3. Turn on Play sound for notifications.
  4. In your Focus settings, allow Calendar to notify you during work hours.
  5. In the Calendar app, set default alerts to a few minutes before events.

This is a real improvement and costs nothing. For many people it's enough.

Step 2: use an alert that takes over the screen

If you still miss meetings, the problem is attention, not settings. A full-screen alert covers every display, so you can't miss it, and stays until you click or press a key.

MeetingFlash is a free, open-source menu bar app that does this:

  • Flashes every display red right before a meeting, showing the meeting title, until you click or press a key.
  • Lets you change the colour, or use a photo instead.
  • Supports several alerts per meeting, for example a soft sound 5 minutes before and the flash at the start.
  • Skips all-day, cancelled and declined events.
  • Lists upcoming meetings with a one-click join for Teams, Zoom, Meet and Webex.
Install
brew install --cask frugoman/tap/meeting-flash
Requires macOS 14 or later. Or download the zip.

Works with Outlook, Google and iCloud

MeetingFlash reads the macOS Calendar app, not each service separately. Add your Exchange, Microsoft 365, Google or iCloud account under System Settings, Internet Accounts and turn on Calendars; its events then show up. It can't see the Outlook app's own private calendar store, so if you only use Outlook, add the account to macOS too.

Keep it from being annoying

  • Sound only where it's OK. Restrict sound to chosen outputs (say your AirPods) so the laptop speakers stay silent in the office.
  • Wi-Fi rules. Mute on named networks, such as the office. Reading the Wi-Fi name needs Location permission, which macOS requires.
  • Pick calendars. Watch only the ones that matter.

FAQ

Does it work with Outlook or Google Calendar?

Yes, through the macOS Calendar app (see above).

Is it free?

Yes, free and MIT licensed. The source is on GitHub.

Does it send my calendar anywhere?

No. It reads your calendar on your Mac and doesn't need an account.
